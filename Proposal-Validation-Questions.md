# Proposal Validation Questions

Use these questions to test whether the 3D clothing simulation proposal is clear, realistic, and strong enough for funding.

1. What exact problem does this project solve?
2. How serious is this problem for online shoppers?
3. How serious is this problem for e-commerce business owners?
4. What evidence shows that customers really care about fit, size, and style before buying?
5. Why is a 3D clothing simulation better than static product images?
6. What makes our solution different from existing online shopping tools?
7. Who are the primary users of this platform?
8. Which customer group is more likely to pay for it first?
9. Are we targeting shoppers, businesses, or both at the same time?
10. Why would a start-up store choose our platform?
11. Why would an established brand switch to our platform?
12. What value does the product give to shoppers?
13. What value does the product give to business owners?
14. How does the platform increase trust in online shopping?
15. How does the platform reduce product returns?
16. How does the platform increase sales conversion?
17. What features are essential in the first version?
18. Which features can be delayed to a later version?
19. Can we actually build the 3D simulation with our current skills and resources?
20. What technical tools or libraries will we need?
21. How will the avatar creation work?
22. How will clothing items be rendered on the avatar?
23. How will we handle different sizes and body types?
24. How will color changes and outfit previews work?
25. Will this be a web app, mobile app, or both?
26. What is the best business model: subscription, one-time license, or custom service?
27. Should we sell source code or charge monthly fees?
28. Which model is more suitable for small businesses?
29. Which model is more suitable for large businesses?
30. How will the project generate revenue over time?
31. What costs are required to build and maintain the system?
32. How much funding do we need at the beginning?
33. Why should a bank or investor trust this project?
34. What risks could make the project fail?
35. How can we reduce those risks?
36. What competitors already offer similar solutions?
37. Why should users choose our solution instead of competitors?
38. How will we test whether the idea is valid?
39. What would success look like after the first prototype?
40. What is the long-term vision for this product?

Answer:

1. The project solves the problem of online shoppers being unable to judge clothing fit, size, and appearance before purchase.
2. It is a serious problem because fashion shopping depends heavily on confidence in fit and style, and uncertainty often leads to hesitation or abandonment.
3. It is serious for business owners because product returns, low conversion, and customer distrust directly reduce profit and increase operational costs.
4. Strong evidence comes from the fact that fashion products have some of the highest return rates in e-commerce, especially when size or fit is misunderstood.
5. A 3D clothing simulation is better than static images because it gives customers a more realistic and interactive preview of how the item looks on a body-like avatar.
6. Our solution is different because it is designed to be simple, affordable, and easy to integrate into online stores, especially for small and medium businesses.
7. The main users are online shoppers and e-commerce business owners.
8. The first group likely to pay is small and medium fashion businesses because they need affordable tools that can improve sales and reduce returns.
9. We are targeting both shoppers and businesses, but the first commercial focus will be on businesses that want to improve their store experience.
10. A startup store would choose our platform because it offers a modern shopping experience without requiring a large budget or complex technical setup.
11. An established brand would switch because the platform helps improve brand image, customer engagement, and conversion rates with a premium digital experience.
12. The product gives shoppers more confidence, better decision-making, and less risk when buying clothes online.
13. The product gives business owners higher conversion, lower return rates, and a stronger competitive position.
14. The platform increases trust by showing a more realistic and transparent representation of how clothing fits and looks.
15. It reduces returns by helping customers better understand size, fit, and appearance before checkout.
16. It increases sales conversion by making shoppers feel more confident and less uncertain about their purchase.
17. The essential first version should include avatar creation, clothing preview, size switching, color changes, and web integration.
18. Advanced physics, full body scanning, AR features, and highly detailed animations can be delayed to later versions.
19. Yes, we can build the first version with our current skills if we focus on a simple MVP and use existing 3D libraries and web tools.
20. We will need tools such as Three.js, Blender, React, Node.js, and possibly a backend framework for web integration.
21. Avatar creation will work through simple body presets, adjustable measurements, and optional template-based customization.
22. Clothing items will be rendered by mapping 3D garment models onto the avatar with basic movement and view controls.
23. We will handle different sizes and body types by using size templates, body measurement inputs, and scalable avatar adjustments.
24. Color changes and outfit previews will work through a simple interface where users can switch colors, styles, and outfit combinations.
25. The first version should be a web app, with mobile support considered later.
26. The best business model is a combination of subscription and custom service, with subscription being the main model for small businesses.
27. We should start by offering monthly or yearly subscriptions, while also offering source code or custom licensing for larger clients.
28. A subscription model is more suitable for small businesses because it is affordable and easier to start with.
29. A custom service or license model is more suitable for large businesses because they may need deeper integration and control.
30. Revenue can come from monthly subscriptions, setup fees, enterprise licensing, and ongoing support services.
31. The main costs are software development, 3D asset creation, hosting, maintenance, testing, and marketing.
32. The initial funding should cover the first MVP, pilot testing, and basic deployment, and a modest amount would be enough to start.
33. A bank or investor should trust the project because it addresses a real commercial problem, has a clear value proposition, and offers a realistic path to revenue.
34. The main risks are technical complexity, limited 3D content quality, slow user adoption, and competition from larger platforms.
35. We can reduce these risks by building a simple MVP first, testing with real users, and using existing tools instead of overcomplicating the first version.
36. Competitors include AR try-on services, virtual fitting room platforms, and 3D fashion visualization tools.
37. Users should choose our solution because it is more practical, more affordable, and easier to integrate into everyday online stores.
38. We will test the idea through a prototype, user feedback, and a pilot with one or more small online stores.
39. Success after the first prototype would mean a working demo, positive user feedback, and evidence that the platform improves confidence and engagement.
40. The long-term vision is to grow the platform into a scalable fashion technology solution for virtual try-on, digital fitting, and personalized online shopping.
